The Supreme Court of the Philippines has officially released the long-anticipated Rules on Electronic Notarization, pursuant to A.M. No. 24-10-14-SC, marking a significant milestone in modernizing legal services. This move will enhance efficiency, accessibility, and security in notarization processes, aligning with global best practices.

The Shift to Digital Notarization

With the Rules on Electronic Notarization now approved formally, businesses, legal professionals, and individuals can expect a more streamlined approach to notarization. The Supreme Court, through extensive consultations with stakeholders has crafted a framework that ensures the validity, security, and compliance of electronic notarized documents. As early as 2023, UNAWA actively engaged with the Supreme Court to provide insights and solutions, contributing to developing a secure and efficient electronic notarization system.

Among the key provisions is the Guidelines on the Accreditation of Electronic Notarization Facility Providers, allowing authorized solutions to offer compliant and secure digital notarization services.

Legal Foundations and Implementation

The legal framework for electronic notarization is rooted in the Electronic Commerce Act of 2000 (Republic Act No. 8792), which recognizes the validity of electronic documents and signatures. The Supreme Court’s newly issued rules build upon this foundation, incorporating lessons from past regulations such as the 2020 Interim Rules on Remote Notarization of Paper Documents and international best practices.

Under A.M. No. 24-10-14-SC, electronic notarization will involve:

  1. Digital document preparation – Ensuring compliance with security standards.
  2. Electronic identity verification – Using AI-powered authentication and facial recognition.
  3. Secure e-signatures and seals – Encrypted to prevent fraud.
  4. Real-time online notarial acts – Allowing remote notarization within legal parameters.
  5. A centralized notarial registry – Providing long-term, verifiable records.

The Supreme Court’s resolution outlines a comprehensive accreditation system for electronic notarization facility providers, ensuring that all digital notarization services adhere to strict legal and security requirements. The Court emphasized its commitment to fostering technological innovation while safeguarding public trust in notarized documents.
