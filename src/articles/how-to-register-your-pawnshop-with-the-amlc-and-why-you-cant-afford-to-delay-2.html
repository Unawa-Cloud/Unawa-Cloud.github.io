What Is the AMLC and Why Does It Matter?

The Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC) is the government body responsible for implementing the country’s anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing laws. It was established under Republic Act No. 9160, also known as the Anti-Money Laundering Act of 2001 (AMLA).

The AMLC serves as the primary financial intelligence unit of the Philippines. It investigates suspicious transactions, works with international watchdogs, and ensures that businesses handling money or high-value assets are not used as vehicles for criminal activity.

Pawnshops, along with banks, money service businesses, and other financial entities, are required to register with the AMLC. They are considered Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions (DNFBPs)—a category that includes businesses handling cash or valuables on behalf of clients.

Failure to register isn’t just a paperwork issue. It means the AMLC cannot monitor your transactions, which puts you in violation of national law. That could lead to fines, legal trouble, or even business closure.

Step-by-Step AMLC Registration Guide for Pawnshops

Step 1: Visit the AMLC Portal

Start by going to the AMLC’s official registration portal: https://portal.amlc.gov.ph. Click the “Sign Up Now” button to begin.

Step 2: Fill Out the Online Registration Form

Enter details about your pawnshop, including business name and registration number, office address, and a list of operating branches.

Step 3: Upload Required Documents

Prepare the following: business permit, list of branches, proof of AMLA seminar attendance for officers, and designation letters for CO, AP, and PDO.

Step 4: Verify Your Email

After submitting the form, confirm the verification email within 72 hours to continue processing.

Step 5: Wait for the Provisional Certificate

Once approved, AMLC issues a Provisional Certificate of Registration (PCOR), valid for six months.

Step 6: Receive Your Final Certificate

After further verification, AMLC issues the Certificate of Registration (COR) via email.

Reminders and Common Issues

- Submissions after 1:00 PM are processed the next business day.
- Save the reference number provided during your application.
- Incomplete or inconsistent documents often cause delays.
- DNFBPs like pawnshops may face additional documentation requests.

After Registration: Ongoing Compliance Matters

Registering with the AMLC is just the start. Once registered, your pawnshop is expected to:
- Conduct Customer Due Diligence (CDD)
- Perform ongoing risk monitoring
- Submit Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs)
- Maintain proper recordkeeping and perform internal audits
- Train staff in AML compliance procedures
