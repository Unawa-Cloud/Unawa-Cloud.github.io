The 2-Minute AML Compliance Test

To gauge whether your law firm meets the requirements of AML compliance, answer the following questions. Be honest—your firm's reputation may depend on it.

Do you have a written AML policy? Every law firm must have a documented policy outlining how it identifies, assesses, and mitigates money laundering risks. Without this, compliance is impossible to demonstrate during regulatory audits.

 

Do you screen clients against sanctions lists? Screening ensures your firm isn't inadvertently working with sanctioned entities or individuals. Regular updates to these lists mean client checks shouldn't stop at onboarding.

 

Do you regularly update client risk profiles? Risks change over time. Has your firm implemented a process to reassess client activities and adjust risk profiles accordingly?

 

Do you promptly report suspicious activities? Law firms must report suspicious transactions to the Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC). Failing to do so can result in penalties.

 

Has your staff received AML training in the past year? Regulations evolve, and it is essential to ensure your team knows current legal requirements to maintain compliance.

 

If you answered "no" to any of these questions, your law firm has compliance gaps that need immediate attention.

What Non-Compliance Looks Like

Failing the test may highlight one or more weaknesses common to many law firms. Here's a closer look at why firms often fall short:

  1. No Written Policies: Some firms operate informally, relying on unwritten procedures that can't hold up to scrutiny.
  2. Inconsistent Screening: A lack of automated tools leads to irregular checks, exposing firms to high-risk clients.
  3. Neglected Monitoring: Too often, firms overlook revisiting client risk profiles after initial onboarding.
  4. Insufficient Reporting: Fear of reputational harm can discourage reporting, but failing to comply with reporting obligations is a violation.
  5. Outdated Training: Staff unaware of updated regulations are more likely to miss red flags, putting the firm at risk.

How to Close the Gaps in AML Compliance

If your firm didn't pass the test, don't worry—there's a clear path to compliance. Start by systematically addressing each gap.

First, draft or revise your AML policy. It should clearly outline how your firm identifies risks, conducts due diligence, monitors clients, and handles suspicious transactions. Ensure that the policy reflects the latest updates to anti-money laundering in the Philippines.

Second, adopt a rigorous client screening process. This means checking clients against up-to-date sanctions lists and conducting adverse media screening. Automated solutions can save your team time and ensure accuracy.

Third, make monitoring a continuous process. Assign accountability within your team to update client profiles regularly. Reassess high-risk clients more frequently to address any red flags.

Finally, invest in your people. AML training should be mandatory for all staff. Regular workshops or seminars help your team stay informed about the evolving regulatory landscape.
